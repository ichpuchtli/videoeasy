# videoeasy story room

The story room is a conversation over a document, not a retrieval problem. A
short documentary's whole record (an hour or so of transcript plus every
annotated shot and window) fits in one context. Read it, hold it, argue
about it.

## Read before speaking

Every session, in this order (paths under `data/films/<film>/`):

1. `out/bible-context.md`: machine-generated facts. Every shot and window
   (subject, movement, metaphors, themes, mood, light, palette, hold time,
   cut notes, motion, audit evidence) and the full word-timestamped
   transcript with client selects marked.
2. `out/bible-synthesis.md`: the judgment layer from earlier sessions, if it
   exists. Start from it. Later builds may sit beside it
   (`bible-synthesis-v2.md`): read the latest too, and treat a build marked
   "proposal" as argument, not decision, until the editor has read it.
3. **The film brief** the editor keeps privately in the film directory: who
   and where, the story spine from the editor's draft radio cut, and
   editorial intent already recorded.
4. **The register reference** the editor keeps privately in the film
   directory: the tone and pacing the film is aiming for.
5. `editorial/intent.json`: the active decisions (`uv run python -m
   videoeasy.intent --film data/films/<film> --cut <cut>` prints the
   resolved brief).

If the brief or register reference is missing, ask the editor for them
before proposing structure.

### What a register reference should contain

A short document (one or two pages) the editor writes once per film or
series. It describes; it does not supply phrases to copy:

- **The reference point**: a programme, filmmaker or past film whose register
  this film borrows, and what specifically is borrowed (warmth, pace,
  plainness, humour) and what is not.
- **Pacing**: typical shot holds, how long a voice runs before the picture
  changes, how much uncovered talking head is welcome, where silence is used.
- **Picture–word relationship**: whether the picture shows what the speaker
  names, supports mood, or deliberately contrasts; when the speaker's face is
  the picture.
- **Voice and address**: who speaks to camera, who is interviewed, whether
  there is narration, and how presenters hand over.
- **Structure habits**: cold open or not, chapter shape, how a segment
  ends, how long the film runs.
- **What to avoid**: register failures specific to this film (sentimentality,
  jargon, a word a subject asked to avoid; the latter also goes in
  `intent.json`).

Put the one-paragraph summary of it in `film.yaml` `context:`. That is what
the prompts carry. Never put example phrases there.

Contact sheets are in `work/sheets/<id>.jpg`. When a specific shot matters to
an argument, **open the sheet and look at it**. A shot id plus a one-line
annotation is not enough to stake an editorial claim on. Grading a whole cut
is different: that goes through the local models (below).

## The two files, and why they are separate

- `bible-context.md` is regenerated by `videoeasy assemble`. Anything written
  there is destroyed on the next ingest run.
- `bible-synthesis.md` is the relationship layer (themes ↔ shots ↔ beats,
  motifs, the voices, coverage gaps), built in conversation, corrected by the
  editor and **persisted**. It is the only durable record of judgment.

Corrections go in the synthesis file, in prose, with the reasoning attached.
Judgment can be wrong, so write it so a future session can disagree with it.
A rebuild is a new file beside the old one, never written over it. Point the
tools at it with `--synthesis` (`storycheck` takes its spine from the section
headed "6. Proposed spine"). Time a spine at word level from
`out/transcripts.json`, not from segment starts.

## Prior editorial intent is data, not a starting suggestion

Already extracted into the bible and already argued over:

- Transcript docx markup: **all colours mean keep**, and the designated
  client colour means the client added it.
- Anchored comments: reorder moves, story-readiness gates, coverage notes,
  cut flags.
- The editor's draft radio cut: the story spine.

Do not re-derive these or quietly overrule them. If your reading of the
footage conflicts with a recorded decision, say so and make the case. When
the editor or a subject decides something, add a rule to `intent.json` with
who, the source, the date and what it supersedes. Never encode a decision
only in a beat note or a prompt.

## Working honestly with the material

- Whisper's wording differs slightly from the human transcript. Marked
  passages are **fuzzy-matched** onto timecodes, so treat those timecodes as
  approximate and say so when it matters.
- A radio cut selects **words**, not segments. The transcript is word-level
  for exactly this reason.
- Report coverage gaps plainly. A beat with no B-roll is the most useful thing
  you can find. Do not paper over it by stretching an unrelated shot.
- Shots annotated as neutral coverage are neutral coverage. Do not inflate a
  fence post into a metaphor for resilience because a beat needs filling.
- What was heard on a clip is not what is visible in it. A spoken topic
  never proves a picture.
- Recording-role seconds are not speaker seconds. A two-shot holds two
  people.

## Typical asks

- "Which B-roll serves this beat?"
- "Build a 13-minute radio cut honouring the client's keeps."
- "What beats have no coverage?"
- "Show me the contact sheet for that shot."
- "Where does the method voice take over from the portrait voice?"

## Judging a cut: measure it, do not eyeball it

When a render, a grade or a cut needs validating, the frontier model does not
open frames and judge by eye. It writes the prompt, sends graded frames to
the local vision model and the transcript to the local text model, reads the
scores, and turns low scores into fixes in the generator. The design is in
`docs/eval-design.md`. One iteration (`F=data/films/<film>`):

```bash
# 0. once per film, before proposing: measurements the judge cannot make from stills
uv run videoeasy transcribe --config config.<film>.yaml --broll
uv run python -m videoeasy.steadiness --config config.<film>.yaml
uv run python -m videoeasy.moves --config config.<film>.yaml
# 1. score the render: picture vs words, exposure, rhythm, joins, cover rules, levels, viewer read
uv run python -m videoeasy.cuteval --cut $F/editorial/cut-vN.json --render $F/editorial/renders/<r>.mp4 \
    --transcripts $F/out/transcripts.json --out $F/editorial/eval/cut-vN-core-auto --frames-dir $F/work/eval/cut-vN-core
# 2. propose cover per spoken pick (text model, ~85 s/beat), then verify each candidate (vision model)
uv run python -m videoeasy.brollmatch --film $F --cut editorial/cut-vN.json --out editorial/broll-plan-vN+1.json \
    --eval editorial/eval/cut-vN-core-auto.json --skip-verify
uv run python -m videoeasy.brollmatch --film $F --cut editorial/cut-vN.json --out editorial/broll-plan-vN+1.json --verify-only
# 3. rebuild the video rows from the verified plan (audio picks unchanged)
uv run python -m videoeasy.cutbuild --film $F --cut editorial/cut-vN.json --plan editorial/broll-plan-vN+1.json --version N+1
# 4. lay in, read back, conform, render (videoeasy-resolve skill)
uv run python -m videoeasy.layin --film $F --cut editorial/cut-vN+1.json --name "<film> cut-vN+1 (AI)" --render <stem>
# 5. story check: did every word play, and does the story hold against the spine and the intent
uv run python -m videoeasy.storycheck --film $F --cut editorial/cut-vN+1.json --render editorial/renders/<stem>.mp4 \
    --out editorial/eval/cut-vN+1-story
```

What the scores mean, and what trips a fix:

- **Picture vs words, 0–3** per stretch, duration-weighted. 3 means the shot
  shows the named thing, 2 the right place or mood, 1 generic, 0 a mismatch
  or unwatchable. Every 0 is a fix, and a beat averaging under 1.5 needs new
  cover. Bare stretches: 3 when the words are personal and the face carries
  them, 1 when they name something showable. The rubric favours literal
  illustration, so weigh purposeful contrast and breathing holds yourself.
- **Exposure:** YAVG under 45 or over 185, clipped, crushed or low
  saturation, measured on the graded render frames.
- **Rhythm:** bare % of spoken time, longest bare run, jump cuts (a
  heuristic that over-reports).
- **Joins:** any black is a lay-in fault, as is silence over 0.15 s.
- **Cover rules (measured):** talking cover, shake outside steady runs, drone
  stretches that are not a clean move. Listed whatever the score says.
- **Levels:** picks more than 3 LU under −24 LUFS or over −1 dBTP; mono picks
  differing left/right by more than 3 LU make the render `broken`.
- **Viewer read:** text only. It does not know who is speaking unless the
  words say so.
- **Story check:** every pick's words as token edits. Any edit on a polarity,
  quantity, modality, name or pronoun token flags the pick. Flagged picks get
  a padded recheck and a waveform test: `cleared_on_recheck`, `confirmed`,
  `waveform_intact`, `intent_mismatch` (fix the pick text) or `unresolved`
  (listen). Nothing is trimmed on one pass. Then the recorded intent, seconds
  per recording role, and the story model's spine table.
- **Intent:** a `broken` rule stops `cutbuild` from writing, and `layin`
  exits 2 on a broken read-back.

Evidence rules:

- Quote the render sha with any score. A score without its manifest is
  unattributable.
- A check that failed or did not run is `unchecked`, not clean. An unjudged
  stretch is outside the mean and listed. An unchecked plan row becomes a
  provisional bare row.
- Frames and transcripts are cached under the render's content hash, so a
  re-render at the same path is never judged on old evidence.
- Tools refuse to overwrite a previous report. Use a new `--out` stem.
- Two scorecards from different methods are not comparable, and a 0.05 change
  in the mean is noise until variance has been measured.

Do not run the proposer (Ollama) and the scorer (LM Studio) at the same
time.

## Scope

The loop changes pictures only. Audio picks (the radio cut) change by hand in
conversation, and the result must pass the same checks. Laying into Resolve
happens when the editor asks for it; never start building timelines just
because a cut looks finished.
